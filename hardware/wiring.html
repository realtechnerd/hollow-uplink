Field Station Wiring

An Arduino Uno and six modules from the kit. Run 5V and GND from the Uno to the breadboard's power rails, power every module from those rails, and run one signal wire from each module straight to the Uno. That's 20 jumper wires.

Leave the MQ-2's DO pin unconnected. For the hatch, the crash sensor (lever switch) needs no magnet. A reed switch or Hall sensor also works on D3 with the same three wires, but both need a small magnet on the lid.

Before you power it

  • Match pins by the label printed on each module, not by position. Keyes boards don't all use the same pin order, and on some the middle pin is +. On the 18B20 and the DHT11, swapping + and − ruins the sensor.
  • Over USB, the 5V rail can power everything: about 315 mA in total, against roughly 500 mA available. The MQ-2 heater (~150 mA) and the relay coil (~80 mA) are most of it. Plug straight into the laptop, not an unpowered hub, and don't power the kit's 3 W LED module from the Uno.
  • The MQ-2 gets warm to the touch. That's its heater working.
  • The Uno's small LED marked L copies the relay state. To switch something you can see, wire an LED with a 220 Ω resistor through the relay's COM and NO terminals.
  • The modules have pins, not sockets, so use female-to-male jumper wires, about 20 cm long, to reach from inside the box to the breadboard.
  • If the hatch or relay reads backwards, change HATCH_CLOSED_LEVEL or RELAY_ON_LEVEL at the top of the sketch. You don't need to rewire.
  • To check the wiring, upload the sketch and open the Serial Monitor at 9600 baud. You should see one JSON line every 2 s, then # gas baseline after 60 s.

Build the box

The box stands in for one zone of the surface. The sensors go inside, the Uno and breadboard stay outside on the bench, and the wires pass through one hole in the side.

Scroll the drawing sideways to see the labels.

Steps

  1. Make the cable hole. Cut or drill a hole about 1.5 cm wide, low on one side of the box. All the sensor wires leave through it.
  2. Mount the MQ-2 high on the inside wall, since smoke rises. Hold it by its red board with tape, Blu-Tack or hot glue, and keep the warm metal can from touching the plastic.
  3. Mount the DHT11 and the 18B20 lower on the same wall. They only need to be inside the box.
  4. Put the crash sensor on the rim so the closed lid pushes its lever down. Lift the lid and the lever springs up, which reads as "hatch open". Test it before gluing.
  5. Put the photoresistor on top of the lid, facing up, and run its wire over the edge and into the cable hole. Covering it with a hand makes a dust storm.
  6. Poke 3 or 4 small vent holes high on the opposite side so smoke clears out between takes.
  7. Run every wire through the hole to the breadboard, then wire it all up as in the diagram above. The relay and its LED or fan sit on the bench, where the camera can see them.
  8. Optional: decorate the lid as a dome or outpost with cardboard and paint, leaving the photoresistor uncovered.

You need

  • A clear plastic food container, 1 to 2 litres. A shoebox works too. A small box makes the smoke reading jump faster.
  • About 20 female-to-male jumper wires, roughly 20 cm long
  • Tape, Blu-Tack or a hot glue gun
  • Scissors or a craft knife for the holes
  • An LED and a 220 Ω resistor, or a small 5V fan, for the relay to switch (optional)
  • Matches or hand sanitizer on a cotton ball for the demo. Never use both at the same time.
